AI automation for Austin small businesses

We find the repetitive work eating your week, then set up assistants and automations that handle it, on the software you already pay for.

What AI automation actually means for a small business

Forget the robots. For a business with five or thirty people, AI automation mostly means this: information arrives somewhere, something reads it and decides what kind of thing it is, and the next step happens without anyone retyping it.

A form becomes a calendar booking. An emailed bill becomes an entry in your accounting software. A rambling voicemail becomes a two-line summary and a drafted reply waiting for your approval. None of this needs a new platform or an in-house technical person. It needs someone to connect the tools you have and test them properly against your real work.

What we set up

  • Lead follow-up. Web forms and missed calls get a fast, personal response and a way to book time with you.
  • Quotes and proposals. Job details become a first draft in your format, ready for you to check and send.
  • Inbox triage. Email gets sorted, summarized and routed, with replies drafted for the routine messages.
  • Bills and invoices. Paperwork gets read and filed in your accounting software, and late payers get a polite reminder.
  • Internal answers. A private assistant answers staff questions from your own procedures, price lists and documents.
  • Reports. The weekly numbers you piece together by hand arrive on their own, with a plain-English summary.
  • Team training. Your people learn how to use AI tools well, and what never to paste into them.

How to tell if something is worth automating

The best first projects share three traits. They happen often, they follow roughly the same pattern each time, and a mistake is cheap to catch. Answering a new lead fits. Deciding whether to fire a client doesn’t.

We’ll tell you when a task should stay with a person, and when a simple rule does the job without AI. Plenty of good automation has no AI in it at all, and that’s usually cheaper and more reliable. Our guide on where to start with AI walks through how to choose, and the busywork calculator helps you put a number on it.

How a project runs

  1. Conversation. We talk through your week and where the time goes.
  2. Short list. You get a few candidate projects, in order of payoff, with the cost and tools spelled out.
  3. Build and test. We set up one project and run it alongside your existing process until it earns your trust.
  4. Hand over and look after. Your team learns it, and we keep it working as your tools change.

Keeping you in control

AI gets things wrong, sometimes confidently. So we design for that. Anything sent under your name can wait for your approval, each automation only gets access to what it needs, and we choose business-grade services with clear terms on how your data is handled. If you want this written down for your staff, start with our free AI policy template. Texas businesses should also know how the state’s AI rules apply.

Common questions

Do we need to replace our current software?

Usually not. We start with what you already use, such as your email, calendar, accounting software and CRM, and connect them. We only suggest something new when the payoff is clear.

What happens when an automation makes a mistake?

We plan for it. Customer-facing steps can require approval, unusual cases get passed to a person, and activity is logged so a problem can be traced and fixed.

Will this replace our staff?

That isn’t the goal. Small teams usually use the time they get back for the work they’ve been putting off, like following up with customers or doing the job itself.

Can you work with us in person?

Yes. We’re based in Austin and happy to come to you, which often helps because we can watch how the work really gets done.
